# Starryear-EyeEye｜可可爱爱眼眼睛睛

Create one finished vertical 9:16 editorial collage. The upper region is the user's original photograph, preserved pixel-faithfully and changed only by cropping, scaling, and placement. The lower region translates the same photograph into a quiet watercolor habitat where source shapes become independently readable soft life-forms, each with exactly one minimal pair of eyes. Keep the surreal intensity low.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the source and internally map its decisive masses, negative spaces, light path, depth, motion, palette, and material behavior. If a style reference is supplied, use only its abstract traits, never its subjects, layout, colors, text, logo, signature, or watermark.
2. Build a single 9:16 canvas. Place a crop-only version of the unchanged source photo above, normally 27–38% of total height; never regenerate, repaint, stylize, extend, or add eyes to this evidence panel. Reserve the remaining height before generating or placing the lower artwork.
3. Segment source-supported shapes for the lower interpretation. Enforce `one shape = one creature = one pair of eyes`; merge noisy micro-detail into larger source-faithful relations. Let each body's silhouette inherit its source contour—rounded fruit, feathery foliage, tapered shadow, branching or trailing form—rather than forcing every creature into a circle or capsule. Preserve enough geometry, clustering, surface cues, and visual weight to recognize focal prototypes.
4. Preserve the source's direction, visual center, depth order, light, palette, and material tendencies below. Establish a clear focal hierarchy instead of giving every creature equal weight. When the photograph has an obvious subject, enlarge its translated creature or cluster until it is the first read, while keeping secondary forms quieter and airier. Build gentle tension through this focal scale contrast, directional flow, and cluster-versus-breathing-space; avoid ominous mass. Introduce only low surrealism through one or two quiet relational operations.
5. Render the lower 58–66% with vivid source-derived watercolor concentration, wet bloom, pastel haze, dry pigment, paper fibers, translucent layers, and soft gaps. Preserve luminous mid-to-high chroma and selected dense pigment pools; do not wash the source palette into pale beige. Use a collage transition rather than a visible frame.
6. Give every lower creature two small dark pigment marks only. Deliberately distribute round dots, flat ovals, closed short dashes, half-closed marks, subtle high-low pairs, and translucent veiled pairs according to body shape and depth; do not let one default eye form dominate. Add no mouth, nose, brows, eye whites, lashes, or full face.
7. Protect the entire lower composition during assembly. Design focal subjects inside the lower panel's safe area; if a chosen vertical artwork already exists, fit it completely into the reserved lower region and extend only its peripheral paper/color field sideways. Never crop away fruit, eyes, bodies, or meaningful bottom content merely to fill width. Return exactly one flattened vertical 9:16 image.

## Guardrails

- Derive all major bodies, colors, light, and motion from the source photograph; a style reference never supplies content or palette.
- Keep surreal additions sparse and relational: use at most one or two source-supported changes in scale, depth, transparency, continuity, or gravity.
- In the generated region, stylize every focal subject into the common watercolor creature family. Preserve useful prototype cues, but never retain a pasted photorealistic fragment, generic smooth sphere, or threatening oversized mass.
- When fruit is focal, make one source-faithful fruit or cluster the unmistakable hero of the generated region. Enlarge it substantially but keep it comfortably within the composition. Cartoonize the whole fruit into the shared watercolor creature language—never paste or preserve a photographic fragment—while retaining its prototype's irregular scale-like facets, seams, wrinkles, clustered attachment, granulation, dry-brush breaks, warm highlights, and darker sediment. Balance recognition and abstraction: neither a literal fruit cutout nor a generic smooth ball. Keep its browns warm, layered, and luminous rather than near-black.
- Every independent creature has exactly one pair of eyes; no floating eyes, shared bodies with multiple pairs, eyeless creatures, cloned expressions, or realistic eyeballs.
- Preserve the upper photograph exactly apart from crop, scale, and placement. Use no filter, reconstruction, retouching, generative fill, eye marks, or painted overlay inside it.
- Decide the final panel geometry before image generation. Do not compose a standalone portrait and then sacrifice its lower focal content during 9:16 assembly.
- Add no unrelated people, hands, realistic animals, props, text, numbers, titles, logos, signatures, watermarks, borders, or hard panel dividers.
